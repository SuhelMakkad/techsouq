Offer an appointment start only when the entire service fits inside your customer-facing hours and every required person, room, and piece of equipment is free for the time it is needed, including setup and cleanup. Apply your notice rules before showing the slot and check availability again when the customer confirms. A free technician cannot take a booking that also needs an occupied room.

This is a rule you can bring to a booking software demo or give to a developer. Start with one service and one representative morning. Write down the times you'd expect the customer to see, then ask the supplier to reproduce them on a test booking page.

## What should the booking system check?

Separate **appointment duration**, **buffer**, and **start interval**. Duration is the time the customer books. A buffer reserves extra time before or after it. The start interval controls which clock times the system considers. A 15-minute interval can offer 09:00, 09:15, and 09:30 as alternative starts for a 40-minute service. Microsoft Bookings documents separate settings for start increments and service length and counts configured buffers when checking staff availability. [Microsoft Bookings scheduling policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/bookings/set-scheduling-policies?view=o365-worldwide#types-of-scheduling-policies), [Microsoft Bookings buffer settings](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/bookings/configure-service-availability?view=o365-worldwide#buffer-time-and-availability)

Give a shared resource its own capacity rule if two services use it. Acuity Scheduling, for example, lets several staff calendars in the same time zone draw from the same limited pool of rooms and treats resources as occupied during configured padding. A supplier demo should show that a room booking for another service can still block yours. [Acuity's guide to limiting bookings by resource](https://help.acuityscheduling.com/hc/en-us/articles/16676949567757-Use-resources-to-limit-bookings)

## Which starts fit in a repair studio's morning?

Imagine a fictional bicycle repair studio with one technician and one work bay shared across services. Both are available during the same 09:00–12:30 local window except for the busy blocks below. A customer fitting takes **40 minutes**, followed by **10 minutes of cleanup** using both the technician and the bay. There is no setup buffer. The studio considers starts every 15 minutes from 09:00, and the sample day already meets the booking notice policy.

- The bay is already reserved from **09:50 to 10:20** for another task.
- The technician is unavailable from **11:15 to 11:30**.
- The fitting and cleanup need **50 uninterrupted minutes** with both resources. Cleanup must end by closing.

For each start, calculate the customer end as **start + 40 minutes**, then the release time as **customer end + 10 minutes**. A busy block conflicts when the start is earlier than the busy block's end **and** the release is later than the busy block's start. A release at 09:50 can touch a reservation starting at 09:50. This example uses the same boundary convention as Google Calendar's free/busy API, which includes the start and excludes the end of a busy block. [Google Calendar free/busy response](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/freebusy/query#response)

The only continuous windows when both are free are 09:00–09:50, 10:20–11:15, and 11:30–12:30. The middle window is 55 minutes long, but its first 15-minute grid start is 10:30, leaving 45 minutes before the technician's break. So the page should offer **09:00 and 11:30**.

## Try a different start interval

Change the example's start interval to **10 minutes** and keep the same fitting, cleanup, and busy blocks. The starts become **09:00, 10:20, 11:30, and 11:40**. At 10:20 the bay has just become free and cleanup ends at 11:10, before the technician's break. The 11:40 fitting uses the last 50 minutes through closing. The 11:30 and 11:40 options overlap, so booking one removes the other.

Use the planner to try your own same-day opening window, service duration, cleanup, and start interval. Enter at most one busy block for each resource in the same local clock time. The planner assumes the technician and room are both required through cleanup. It estimates which alternatives fit those inputs and leaves live calendar checks, setup time, notice, and time zone conversion to the booking system.

## What happens when a customer confirms?

Use one authoritative booking record for each reservation and confirm only after it has reserved all the required capacity. A time can be taken between the availability search and the customer's final click. Square's Bookings API, for example, documents searching for available times and creating a booking as separate operations. Ask the supplier to demonstrate this gap using two customer sessions, with one successful reservation and a clear prompt to find another time in the other session. [Square's availability and booking workflow](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/bookings-api/use-the-api#search-for-available-slots)

If a required calendar is unavailable, treat capacity as unknown until it can be verified. Google's free/busy response can return an error for an individual calendar. Agree whether to pause instant booking for the affected service or accept a separately labelled request for staff to confirm. Pending requests need a clear policy on whether they hold capacity and when that hold expires. [Google Calendar free/busy errors](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/freebusy/query#response)

For a service delivered at a physical venue, use that location's time zone to define recurring hours and show the zone with the date and time on the booking page and confirmation. Test a customer viewing from another zone. For locations that change their clocks seasonally, test a date on either side of the change and decide how to handle any repeated or skipped local times that fall within service hours. The IANA database tracks changes to UTC offsets and daylight-saving rules by location. [IANA Time Zone Database](https://www.iana.org/time-zones)

## What should a supplier demonstrate before launch?

Give the supplier your worked morning and expected starts. Then ask them to use the normal booking page and staff calendar to rehearse the cases that can change availability.

If a hosted scheduler can demonstrate those results for your actual resources and channels, it may be enough to connect it to your website. If separate systems own staff, rooms, and bookings, establish which one can reserve the final slot before deciding whether you need an integration or a custom application.
